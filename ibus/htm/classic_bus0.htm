雙層巴士

本網頁收錄了自一九四九年之後投入各專利巴士公司服務的各類型單、雙層古典巴士，而這些巴士又先後於一九九八年或之前退出了服務行列。

一九四九年是香港巴士服務的一個重要的年頭，從這年起，本港出現了首架雙層巴士，而雙層巴士亦隨之在九龍半島開枝散葉，開花結果，成為九龍市區擔負重任的公共巴士。

至於港島方面，首輛雙層巴士是在一九六二年下半年才出現。接著，雙層巴士亦陸續參與港島北岸主要巴士幹線的服務。踏入七十年代，港島區雙層巴士逐漸取代昔日的各款單層巴士。

研究及探討雙層巴士在本港出現的一頁歷史，是一樁蠻有意思的事情。一九四五年，本港在重光之後，由於淪陷時期日軍已將所有本港儲存的汽油用作軍事用途，港九兩家專利巴士公司的車輛，停用已久。當港島的中華巴士公司和九龍的九龍巴士公司恢復服務之時，要全面進行修理，才能派出數量極之有限的巴士行走。

至一九四七年，為應付交通需求，巴士公司不得不將大貨車改裝，作為巴士的用途，才可以勉強應付，但長遠的解決方法，是要從英國購買新巴士來港。

一九四八年初，九龍巴士公司的總經理雷瑞德先生，親往英國洽購新巴士來港，期間，他看見英國已有雙層巴士在市區行駛。這些雙層巴士的載客量比單層巴士增加了足足一倍，下層車廂又能容納企位乘客。他認為既然是購買新巴士，可以考慮選擇雙層巴士，於是便到生產雙層巴士的廠商去參觀。

去到英國『丹拿』(Dailmer)車廠，詳細研究當年生產中的CVG5型雙層巴士的性能。這款巴士的車門開在車尾，樓梯亦設於車尾。全長26呎，闊7呎6吋，高14 呎6吋。車廂地台距離地面僅1呎半，比使用中的單層巴士還要低，乘客上落更加容易。在試車時，巴士半傾側至35度仍不翻倒。雷瑞德決定訂購二十部，而其中五部現貨底盤先行付運。

一九四八年四月初，九龍巴士召開記者招待會，雷瑞德宣佈購買雙層巴士的嶄新大計。他預計首五輛雙層巴士約於下半年抵港，年底前可望投入服務。但是，當五輛雙層巴士抵港之時，有不少的問題，有待解決。

駕駛雙層巴士不難，換言之司機訓練並非重要。反而，雙層巴士行駛的路段兩旁的招牌及樹木，卻是最大的障礙。當九巴首輛雙層巴士在九龍半島沿著2號線試車，從九龍尖沙咀碼頭開往深水埗，當駛經彌敦道時，路旁的大樹阻礙雙層巴士行駛；而街道兩旁很多商店的招牌，均低於15呎。於是，這些伸出的樹枝要當局派人清除，而港府又要通過法例，限制商店的招牌不得低於雙層巴士的高度。

如此一來，經過多個月的努力，路面上的障礙物才逐步除掉，九龍半島雙層巴士正式投入服務的日期，是一九四九年四月十七日，行走1號路線，往來尖沙咀碼頭與九龍城之間。

雙層巴士大容量的運輸功能，在香港這個地少人多的彈丸之地，完全得以發揮出來。隨著九龍半島及新界區人口不斷增加，九巴先後於六、七十年代大量增購長度達34呎的大型雙層巴士，開創了英國製造的雙層巴士的新標準。

時至今日，雙層巴士更加成為本港這個國際大都市的特色之一。除了英國本土之外，香港市面上的雙層巴士，無論從款式及數量而言，都可以擠身於世界前列之內。

Double-decked Buses

This Website contains all the single and doubled-decked classic buses of the franchised bus companies since 1949. All these buses also were decommissioned before 1998.

The year 1949 was an important one for the history of buses in Hong Kong. From then on, Hong Kong saw the first doubled-decked bus. They then florished on the Kowloon Peninsula and shouldered an important transport link for the Kowloon urban area.

On Hong Kong Island, the first doubled-decked bus appeared in the second half of 1962. Thereafter, they took over a major share of services on the north shore of the island. Into the 1970's, doubled-decked buses replaced most of the previous single-decked ones.

To investigate and explore the history of doubled-decked buses in HK is a meaningful task. During the Japanese occupation in the Second World War, the Japanese army used up all the reserve petrol for military use. The two franchised bus companies were unable to operate for a while. After the liberation of HK in 1945, CMB and KMB on the Island and Kowloon were required to put in much repair work before a very limited number of buses could become operational.

Until 1947, the bus operators converted large lorries into buses so as to satisfy passenger demand. The long-term answer, however, was to purchase buses from England.

In early 1948, Mr. Lui Sui-tak, General Manager of KMB, went to England to acquire new buses running in the city carrying doubled the number of passengers. He decided to pay a visit to the doubled-decked bus manufacturers.

He went to the "Dailmer" plant and studied in detail the CVG5 assembly at that time. This model had the door as well as the stairs at the rear. The overall length was 26 ft, width 7'6" and height 14'6". The cabin floor was only 1'1/2" from the ground and was even lower than the single-decked ones in use. They provided good access for passengers. The buses could be tilted to a maximum of 35 degrees. Mr. Lui decided to order 20 with 5 ready ones shipped first in their chassis form.

In April 1948, KMB called a press conference and Mr. Lui announced the new plan of acquiring doubled-decked buses. He anticipated that the first 5 buses would be here by the end of the year and be operational. Nevertheless by the time the buses came, there were quite a number of problems and they needed time to solve.

For the bus driver, controlling a doubled-decked bus did not pose any problem. The difficulty, however, was the sign-boards and trees along the route. When KMB's first doubled-decked bus travelled on the present route 2 from Star Ferry to Shamshuipo, the large branches along Nathan Road were real hazards. Quite a number of sign-boards were also under 15 ft. and needed the government to pass laws to limit their erection.

It took severals months of hard work before the Kowloon doubled-decked bus service became a reality on 17 April 1949 running on route 1 from Star Ferry to Kowloon City.

The usefulness of doubled-decked buses in carrying a large number of passengers was very apparent in Hong Kong. They were in great demand and KMB brought in a large number of doubled-decked buses in the '60s and '70s, some as long as 34 ft. which subsequently became the standard for British ones.

Still today, doubled-decked buses are a symbol for this metropolitan city. Other than in the UK itself, the doubled-decked buses in Hong Kong can be ranked in the forefront of the world in terms of the style and number.
